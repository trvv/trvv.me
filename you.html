On Things ========= What is a thing? The word itself originates in the protogermanic stem for a discussion, or meeting. In as such a thing is then in its most bare form a topic of discussion, thus its elusivity in definition. How strange is it to describe something as a thing then at all? Couldn't any words or combinations therein be "things?" Of course, that is the common intuition that people come to but thing is a very, very strange word to intuit. Even with this topic-oriented interpretation in mind it is quite perplexing. Nonetheless I find it to be a very relevant way to introduce the thing in its linguistic sense (or discursive sense) as that which can be accessed through language. For something to be a thing (!) it has to be describeable, or it can't be discussed, and thus discussing it is moot (another german word for meeting ha). So we can call all linguistic constructions "things" in the sense that they are indeed linguistic constructors. (I sound insane!) You (doer) are experiencing (does) this (doee). WE can describe a deed as this sum of three parts, some of which may be implied, a doer, a doing, and a doee. These constructions lead to more complicated things (the deeds) which describe actions or events. A deed must be built from these components a "doing" is a kind of incomplete deed or template, though I imagine I will find a better word for this later on. Maybe does. This produces a peculiar formula that is also a truism: doer does doee. We can call this construction a deed. Plainly, a deed is a doer doing a doee. doer does doee -> deed You(doer) feel(does) this(doee) -> deed This(doer) tells(does) you(doee) -> deed Doer does deed. to(doee, does(deed, doer)) We can very often invert constructions in the sense of their activity/passivity. That is that when one action is being performed upon a thing there is a correlative action of the thing that is being performed upon "doing something" in receiving the afformentioned action to the originator. You can always flip around the order of doer/doee as long as you flip the active direction of the sentence as well. In this sense we will reduce our consideration of verbs or actions only to the simple active cases and ignore the passive ones as a matter of principle. -------- This is going to be some loose philisophicalish nonsense for my own entertainment and further clarity of thought. The two basic materials of language are acts (deeds) and objects (things). Acts are, informally, functions with variable arity taking objects as parameters, rendering themselves objects. Perhaps there is some underlying unity to acts and objects (are not all acts interpretable as abstract objects, and objects as a kind of persistent act?) but this distinction is useful for now. When the arity of an act exceeds 1 there becomes necessary a distinction between that object which is acting and that object which is being acted upon, henceforth the subject and the object respectively I will focus on using only language of Anglo-Saxon heritage in explicit or jargonish construction, as to avoid the problematic baggage of the traditional mediterranean imports. This isn't a white supremacist thing, just an impulse to use the words that seem to describe these concepts most "simply" and that don't conflict with words that people will already have some weird things about. Unfortunately I cannot avoid the problematic influence of German philisophical vernacular here, but I think that's alright. So, language is the medium through which we can form certainties that exist outside of us, or to communicate our interpretations of reality to others. We will assume that language at least analogizes reality in that it is specifically a means to reflect it (within itself... oh fractals) and so some insights into how language functions may present in consort some insights into the nature of reality, or at least how our language maps onto reality. As we shall discuss this distinction is not so important as it might seem intuitively given that we are limited in our exoteric capacity to an understanding of reality entirely defined by language. Should we attempt to incorporate the esoteric or internalized notions of reality we are a little more scrambled. An obviousish analogy here is to that of language models, where the input-output tokens are concrete linguistic constructions and the "hidden" semantic space between the two endpoints is definitionally of a higher-dimension and parameter count than the tokens on either end, reflecting that the processing of linguistic (or realityish information) is larger than the language rendered. I will attempt to figure some things out about the world starting from an intuitive argument: you are reading this. It is a strange sort of statement in that your knowledge of the concepts that underly it are going to be used to justify the existence of those things as terms. This is sloppy, but I'd likely die on the hill that it is just about the only tautological statement that can be put to print in a way that a child would easily understand, and possibly see the importance of. To enumerate: there is a you, there is a this, there is a thing called reading happening between these things. So we have things, and we have deeds. We can say with a peculiar degree of certainty that you are, in fact, reading this.
